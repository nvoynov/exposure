Negative Capability

Sometimes strange photographs happen, shot under the influence of a fleeting moment—in a flow of light, sound, motion, and something obscure at the very edge of perception. You look at this image in the editor, vaguely recalling the feeling… the image begs to be thrown into the trash, but something unclear holds you back from deleting and forgetting it. Then you stumble upon it yet again, accidentally apply some tool, and it becomes “interesting”—or, much rarer, “very interesting.”

This entire series began with contemplating one failed result and an accidental click on the negative inversion option. What followed was a search among other “failures” that had miraculously survived deep in the archive and on old memory cards. Miraculously, about a dozen of these underdogs accumulated over several years. Later, I made a few attempts to shoot with a “negative result” in mind from the start—but the outcome was questionable. Still, the direction is intriguing.

What makes us take a strange photograph in the first place? What kind of intuition forces us to keep such an image for years, while the rational mind rejects it time and time again? Perhaps this is precisely what Carlos Castaneda called “a cubic centimeter of chance” that appears from time to time before each of us, and the whole trick is to be alert enough to pluck it.
