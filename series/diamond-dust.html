Diamond Dust

Winter was harshly beautiful and went away slowly and brilliantly. Born as an accidental offshoot of a larger journey, this series captures a fleeting micro-universe discovered frozen underfoot. An unusually snowy winter transformed the melting forest tracks into a transient laboratory of pure form, crystal structures, and trapped air.

Locked within the ice, fallen autumn leaves and fragments of pine bark became ancient inclusions preserved in amber. Above them, clusters of air bubbles assembled into cosmic constellations, working as natural optical lenses that magnify and distort the underlying textures. Stripped of familiar scale, these macro-fragments document a fragile, temporary alignment of ice, air, and organic life—a silent art gallery that dissolved completely within days.

This frozen glass changes the very physics of looking. It forces the eye to wander through an inverted scale where a puddle on a dirt track expands into an uncharted nebula. The trapped air pockets act as primitive clocks, counting down the hours of the thaw. Every crack in the surface is a borderline between seasons, a quiet friction between the rigid geometry of winter and the fluid chaos of coming spring.

Ultimately, these images are records of a double disappearance. The organic debris of the past year is slowly consumed by time, while the icy shroud that immortalized it vanishes into the mud. What remains is only the gaze that managed to halt this transition—if only for a single morning.
